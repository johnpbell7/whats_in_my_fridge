Food storage

How long do eggs last?

Updated 15 October 2026 · 4 min read

Eggs keep 3–5 weeks in the fridge and are best used by the date on the box. Store them in their carton in the main body of the fridge — not the door.

EggsKeepsNotes
Fresh eggs (in shell)3–5 weeksBest by the box date
Hard-boiled (in shell)Up to 1 weekKeep refrigerated
Leftover raw yolk / white2–4 daysCover tightly

The float test

Not sure if an egg is still good? Pop it in a bowl of water. A fresh egg sinks and lies flat; an older-but-fine egg stands on end; a floating egg is past it — bin it. Crack any doubtful egg into a separate bowl first and give it a sniff.

How to store eggs so they last

Keep them in the carton (it stops them absorbing fridge smells and losing moisture) and in the coldest part — the main shelves, not the door where the temperature swings.

Best before, and what that means for eggs

The date on a box of eggs is a best-before date. It is about quality, not a hard safety limit. Eggs a few days past it are often fine, but the fresher they are, the better they cook. If you use eggs after the date, cook them until the white and yolk are firm rather than serving them runny.

Throw away any egg with a cracked shell. Bacteria can get in through the crack.

Do not wash eggs

Leave eggs unwashed. Washing can push bacteria through the shell and strips off its natural protective coating. If an egg has a bit of dirt on it, wipe it with a dry cloth or paper towel, then wash your hands.

Boiled eggs

Hard-boiled eggs keep up to a week in the fridge. Keep them in their shells until you want to eat them, because the shell protects them. Once peeled, put them in a covered container and eat them sooner. Don’t freeze whole boiled eggs, as the whites go rubbery.

Can you freeze eggs?

Not in the shell, because they can crack as they freeze. Instead, crack them into a bowl, beat them lightly and pour them into an ice-cube tray or a small tub. Label it with the date and how many eggs are in it. Use them within about 3 months, thaw them in the fridge and cook them thoroughly. They work well in scrambled eggs, omelettes and baking.

Eggs and food safety

Wash your hands, utensils and worktops after touching raw egg. Keep raw egg away from ready-to-eat food. If you are cooking for a baby, an older person, or anyone pregnant or unwell, and you are not sure the eggs are British Lion eggs, cook them until the white and yolk are solid.
