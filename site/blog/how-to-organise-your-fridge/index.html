How to organise your fridge so nothing gets forgotten

9 June 2026 · 5 min read · Updated July 2026

Organise your fridge by zone: ready-to-eat food and leftovers on the top shelves, dairy and eggs in the middle, raw meat and fish sealed on the coldest bottom shelf, fruit and veg in the drawers, and condiments in the door. Keep the whole thing at 0 to 5°C, and put older items at the front so they get used first.

The one rule that matters most

If you remember nothing else, remember this: ready-to-eat food goes above raw meat, never below it. Raw meat, poultry and fish can drip, and those drips carry bacteria. Anything you will eat without cooking, like cheese, leftovers or salad, needs to be out of the splash zone. The Food Standards Agency covers safe chilling and cross-contamination at food.gov.uk.

Everything else in this guide is about making that easy to stick to, and making sure food gets eaten instead of quietly going off at the back.

Where everything goes, shelf by shelf

A fridge is colder at the bottom and warmer at the top and in the door. Matching each food to the right zone keeps it fresh for longer and keeps you safe.

ZoneWhat goes hereWhy
Top shelvesLeftovers, cooked meats, drinks, yoghurtSteady temperature, and safely above any raw drips
Middle shelvesDairy, cheese, butter, eggsConsistent chill without being the coldest spot
Bottom shelfRaw meat, poultry and fish, sealedColdest part, and nothing can drip onto food below
Salad drawersFruit and vegHigher humidity keeps produce crisp for longer
DoorCondiments, sauces, jams, juiceWarmest part, so only for things that cope with it

Two small things make a big difference. Keep raw meat in a sealed box or on a plate, not loose. And try to keep fruit and veg apart where you can, because some fruit gives off ethylene as it ripens and speeds up everything nearby.

What temperature should your fridge be?

Your fridge should run at 5°C or below, and 0 to 5°C is the sweet spot. The catch is that plenty of home fridges sit warmer than the dial claims, especially if they are packed or the door gets opened a lot. A cheap fridge thermometer on the middle shelf settles the question in a day, and every degree colder (down to 0°C) buys your food a little more time. Create a "use first" zone

Pick one shelf, or just a small box or tray at the front, and make it the spot for anything close to its date or already opened. Whatever lands there is your shortlist for tonight's dinner. It turns "what needs using up?" from a fridge-wide rummage into a two-second glance, which is the difference between food getting cooked and food getting binned.

Don't over-pack it. Air needs to move for a fridge to stay cold. A jammed fridge runs warmer and hides food, the two things you are trying to avoid. Leave a little space around the shelves.

Label and date your leftovers

Leftovers become a mystery within about three days if you cannot remember when you made them. Clear tubs and a quick note of what it is and the date fixes that. On safety, the FSA advice is to cool leftovers within one to two hours, get them in the fridge, use them within two days, and reheat only once until piping hot all the way through. Freeze anything you will not get to in time.

Rotate: oldest at the front

Shops call it "first in, first out", and it works just as well at home. When you unpack the shopping, move older items to the front and put the new ones behind. It sounds fussy, but it is the single habit that stops the yoghurt at the back turning into a science project. Do it once a week when you put the big shop away and it becomes automatic.

Fridge organisation: frequently asked questions

What is the best way to organise a fridge?

Store by zone: raw meat and fish on the coldest bottom shelf, dairy and leftovers on the middle shelves, fruit and veg in the drawers, and condiments in the door. Keep older items at the front so they get used first.

Where should I store raw meat in the fridge?

On the bottom shelf, sealed, so any drips cannot reach other food. The Food Standards Agency recommends this to avoid cross-contamination, and covers safe chilling at food.gov.uk.

What temperature should my fridge be?

At or below 5°C, ideally between 0 and 5°C. Many fridges run warmer than people think, which shortens how long food lasts, so a cheap fridge thermometer is worth having.

Should eggs and milk go in the fridge door?

Keep milk on a shelf rather than the door, since the door is the warmest part and swings in temperature every time you open it. Eggs are best kept in their box on a middle shelf, not the door, so they stay at a steady temperature.
