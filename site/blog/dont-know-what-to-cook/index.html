Don't know what to cook? Beating the daily fridge dilemma

29 June 2026 · 6 min read · Updated July 2026

When you have no idea what to cook, do not reach for a recipe. Pick a method instead. Look for what needs using up first, then drop it into a stir-fry, a pasta, a traybake, a curry, fried rice, an omelette or a soup. That one decision turns a random fridge into dinner in 15 to 35 minutes, no recipe hunt required.

If that 6pm fridge stare feels familiar, you are not lazy and you are not a bad cook. You have simply run out of one thing: decisions.

Why "I don't know what to cook" is so draining

By the time dinner rolls around, you have already made hundreds of small choices, at work, with the kids, on your phone. Psychologists call what is left decision fatigue, and "what's for dinner?" lands right at the bottom of the tank.

The fridge does not help. It is a jumble of half-used ingredients and good intentions, with no obvious plan staring back at you. And in most homes that mental load falls on the same person, night after night, which is exactly why the easy answer becomes a takeaway or beans on toast for the third time this week.

You're not short of food, you're short of a plan

Here is the twist: most of us have far more to work with than we think. The chicken is in the drawer, there is half a bag of spinach wilting at the back, a tin of chickpeas in the cupboard, and enough odds and ends to make something genuinely good. We just shop on autopilot, forget what we bought, and never quite see the meal hiding in the ingredients.

Pick a method, not a recipe

Recipes need a specific list of ingredients. Methods do not. A handful of forgiving methods will absorb almost anything you have, so instead of asking "what recipe can I follow?", ask "which of these fits what is in the fridge?". Find your main ingredient, match it to a row, and you have dinner.

Quick ways to break the deadlock

A few habits take most of the guesswork out of it:

  • Cook what's going off first. Glance for the herbs, the half pepper, the yoghurt nearing its date, and build the meal around those. It is the single best way to eat well and waste less.
  • Lean on a formula. Protein, plus veg, plus a carb, plus something with flavour, equals dinner. More on that in what can I make for dinner.
  • Narrow the field. "Italian", "something with chicken", "quick". Giving yourself one constraint is far easier than facing infinite options.
Keep a few flavour heroes in: garlic, onions, stock cubes, soy sauce, a hard cheese and dried chilli will rescue almost any plate of leftovers.

Deciding what to cook: frequently asked questions

What should I cook when I have no ideas?

Start from what needs using first, then pick a method that fits it: a stir-fry, a pasta, a traybake or a curry stretch almost anything. Or let an app read your ingredients and suggest a few dinners so you skip the deciding.

How do I beat decision fatigue at dinner?

Take the choice off yourself. Narrow it to what you already have, set a mood (quick, comfort, light) and pick from three options rather than the whole internet. Fewer choices, faster decision.

What are some easy dinners when I cannot be bothered?

Lean on a method that forgives whatever you have: a stir-fry, a traybake, a pasta, a curry, fried rice, an omelette or a soup. Each works with a protein and whatever veg is knocking about, and most are on the table in 15 to 35 minutes.
