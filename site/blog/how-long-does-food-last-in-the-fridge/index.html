How long does food last in the fridge?

Updated 3 July 2026 · 6 min read

Half the battle with food waste is simply knowing how long things keep. Here’s a clear UK fridge & freezer storage chart — plus where to store what, and how to stop good food disappearing to the back of the shelf.

First, get your fridge temperature right

Your fridge should sit at or below 5°C (ideally 0–5°C). Loads of fridges run warmer than people realise, which quietly shortens how long everything below lasts. A cheap fridge thermometer settles it in a day.

The fridge & freezer storage chart

Rough, sensible timings once food is opened or cooked — always check the packaging and your own eyes and nose too.

Use by vs best before: “Use by” is about safety — don’t eat it after. “Best before” is about quality — usually fine for a while past it. Tins, pasta and dried goods last months or years, so they shouldn’t be on your “use soon” radar at all.

Where to store what

  • Bottom shelf (coldest) — raw meat & fish, so drips can’t reach anything else.
  • Middle shelves — dairy, leftovers, cooked food.
  • Drawers — fruit and veg.
  • Door (warmest) — condiments and juice, not milk or eggs.
